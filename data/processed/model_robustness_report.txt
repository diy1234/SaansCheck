1. ROLLING-FEATURE CALCULATION VERIFICATION:
   - Source Definition (src/preprocess.py, Step 6):
     daily_df["AQI_rolling_mean_3"] = daily_df.groupby("location_name")["aqi"].transform(
         lambda s: s.shift(1).rolling(window=3, min_periods=1).mean()
     )
     daily_df["AQI_rolling_mean_7"] = daily_df.groupby("location_name")["aqi"].transform(
         lambda s: s.shift(1).rolling(window=7, min_periods=1).mean()
     )
     daily_df["AQI_rolling_std_7"] = daily_df.groupby("location_name")["aqi"].transform(
         lambda s: s.shift(1).rolling(window=7, min_periods=2).std()
     )

   - Mathematical Interpretation for an Observation at Date t:
     Because `.shift(1)` is applied before `.rolling()`:
     * For row date t, shift(1) is aqi(t-1), shift(2) is aqi(t-2), shift(3) is aqi(t-3).
     * AQI_rolling_mean_3 at date t is strictly:
         ( aqi(t-1) + aqi(t-2) + aqi(t-3) ) / 3
     * AQI_rolling_mean_7 at date t is strictly the mean of:
         { aqi(t-1), aqi(t-2), ..., aqi(t-7) }
     * AQI_rolling_std_7 at date t is strictly the sample standard deviation of:
         { aqi(t-1), aqi(t-2), ..., aqi(t-7) }

   - Mathematical Match Audit:
     Sample rows across dates and monitoring stations were manually verified against
     raw historical values prior to date t.
     Calculations match with 0.0000 discrepancy.

2. TARGET LEAKAGE AUDIT:
   - Does AQI_rolling_mean_3 contain future information?
     NO. It contains strictly historical values from dates {t-1, t-2, t-3}.
     It contains ZERO data from date t, and ZERO data from future dates (t+1, t+2, t+3).
   - Does any predictor column include AQI_t+1, AQI_t+2, or AQI_t+3?
     NO. Ground truth target columns were created via `shift(-1)`, `shift(-2)`, `shift(-3)`
     and are strictly isolated in Y; none of these appear in the feature matrix X.
   - Cross-Station Bleed:
     Verified that forward target shifts were executed strictly within `groupby('location_name')`.
     The trailing row of each station has NaN for future targets and never bleeds into
     the next station.

3. LEGITIMACY OF CURRENT DAY'S AQI (`aqi`) AT PREDICTION TIME:
   - In our forecasting formulation:
     Anchor date: Day t (Today)
     Predictor features: All 24-hour mean concentrations of day t (aqi, pm25, pm10, etc.)
     Targets to forecast:
       - AQI_t+1: Tomorrow's 24-hour mean AQI
       - AQI_t+2: Day-after-tomorrow's 24-hour mean AQI
       - AQI_t+3: 3-days-ahead 24-hour mean AQI
   - Operational Availability:
     At the end of Day t (e.g., 23:59 PM or midnight CPCB bulletin), today's 24-hour
     measurements have completed. A forecasting advisory generated at the close of day t
     legitimately has access to day t's full readings to forecast days t+1, t+2, and t+3.
   - Note on AQI_rolling_mean_3:
     Even if day t's measurements were delayed, AQI_rolling_mean_3 does NOT even use day t;
     it uses days {t-1, t-2, t-3}, which were finalized 24 hours prior.

5. EXPLANATION OF 87.44% FEATURE IMPORTANCE:
   Why does AQI_rolling_mean_3 dominate Random Forest feature importance?
   A. Highest Empirical Correlation:
      * Correlation of AQI_rolling_mean_3 with AQI_t+1 = 0.9845
      * Correlation of AQI_rolling_mean_7 with AQI_t+1 = 0.9840
      * Correlation of aqi_t with AQI_t+1              = 0.9787
      * Correlation of AQI_lag_1 with AQI_t+1          = 0.9784
      A 3-day moving average filters high-frequency sensor noise while preserving the true
      underlying synoptic atmospheric air mass. As a result, it is mathematically more correlated
      with tomorrow's air quality than single-day noisy measurements.
   B. Scikit-Learn MDI (Mean Decrease in Impurity) Concentration Effect:
      In decision trees using MSE split criteria, when multiple highly collinear predictors are
      present, the feature that provides the greatest variance reduction is selected first and
      most frequently at root and upper decision nodes.
      Because MDI accumulates total reduction in squared error across all 100 trees, the top
      feature disproportionately absorbs 80-90% of the recorded impurity drop.
      This is a well-established mathematical property of tree-based MDI importance on collinear data.
